W: Hi Daniel, you look worried. Is everything going well with your internship application?
M: Not really. I sent my resume to a dozen companies, but only two replied, and both turned me down for lack of experience.
W: That's tough, but don't take it personally. Most employers expect students to have limited experience. What matters is how you present what you have already done.
M: Like what? I've only done course projects and a part-time job at a bookstore.
W: That's more than you think. In your course projects, you probably learned teamwork, research skills, and how to meet deadlines. And working at the bookstore taught you customer service and responsibility.
M: I never thought of it that way. I just assumed companies want formal internships on the resume.
W: Some do, but many are looking for potential. Why don't you rewrite your resume to highlight transferable skills? Start each point with an action verb, and add numbers if you can.
M: Numbers? For a bookstore job?
W: Sure. For example, you might say you served eighty customers a day and managed inventory for the weekend rush. Concrete details make you credible.
M: That makes sense. Should I also ask my professor for a recommendation letter?
W: Absolutely. A short letter from someone who knows your work ethic can offset the lack of formal experience. And consider smaller companies too. They often give interns more real responsibility, and your contribution is easier to see.
M: I'll give it a try this weekend. I'll list my course projects first and describe them with action verbs.
W: Good plan. Also, tailor your resume for each position. Mention the company's field and explain why your skills fit it. A generic resume signals a generic candidate.
M: I see. So quality beats quantity when it comes to applications.
W: Exactly. Ten careful applications are worth more than fifty random ones. And remember, rejection is part of the process. The right opportunity usually comes after a few doors close.
M: Thank you so much for the advice. I feel much more confident now.
W: No problem. Good luck with the applications, and let me know how it goes.
